# Commercial Licence

UCM is published under the [AGPL-3.0](LICENSE). This page is for the organisations
that cannot use it on those terms.

## You probably do not need this

The AGPL is not a restriction on using the software. If any of these describes you,
the AGPL already covers it and you owe nothing:

- You run UCM inside your company, for your own certificates.
- You modified it for your own needs and keep those changes to yourself.
- You deploy it for a client and hand over the source you deployed.
- You resell hardware or a service with UCM on it, and your customers can get the
  source of the UCM you shipped them.
- You host UCM for other people and charge them for it, running it as released.

Running UCM in production, at any scale, commercially, is free. That is the point of
the licence.

## When you do need it

The AGPL has one real requirement, and it lives in its section 13: **if you modify**
UCM and offer it to others over a network, those users must be able to get the source
of your version. The obligation comes from modifying, not from hosting and not from
charging: run UCM as released and you owe nothing, whatever you invoice for it.

A commercial licence removes that requirement for the case where you do modify it.
In practice:

- You host a modified UCM as a service for your customers and cannot publish your
  modifications.
- You embed UCM in a product you distribute under closed terms.
- Your legal department forbids AGPL code on the premises, whatever the use.

## One application

The commercial licence unlocks nothing. There is one UCM, the one under the AGPL,
and it is complete: it installs, configures and runs on its own, on a network with
no route to the internet if that is what you have. No activation, no licence server,
no feature held back for paying customers.

What is sold on this page is permission, not capability: the right to use UCM
without the source obligations of the AGPL. Everything UCM does, the AGPL version
does.

- The right to use, modify and distribute UCM without the source obligations of the
  AGPL-3.0, for the entity named in the agreement.
- Every version released while the licence is active, and the right to keep using
  the versions you received after it ends.
- Your feature requests are looked at before the rest of the queue. It does not buy
  a commitment to build them, but it does decide the order.

## What it does not grant

No support commitment and no response deadline. UCM is written by one person, and a
promise of support that cannot be kept is worth less than an honest absence of one.
Bug reports from licence holders go through the same public issue tracker as
everyone else's, where they are answered as fast as anyone's.

If you need contractual support with a guaranteed response time, write and say so.
It can be discussed separately, and it will be priced for what it actually costs.
